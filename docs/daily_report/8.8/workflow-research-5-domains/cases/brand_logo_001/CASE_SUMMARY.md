## Input

Existing London Life "Constellation Managed Portfolios" program, tool, and materials,
following the January 2020 merger of three insurance entities into Canada Life; the new
Canada Life corporate brand identity that legacy materials needed to be brought into
alignment with.

## Verified workflow sequence (8 steps, all EXTERNAL_SOURCE_CONFIRMED)

1. **Define rebrand objectives** — three named objectives: rebrand to new Canada Life
   identity; improve advisor/client resonance; increase advisor awareness/adoption.
2. **Develop client personas** — five personas plus a quantified secondary audience
   ($25,000+ household investable assets, ages 25-50).
3. **Develop advisor personas** — three personas (newer, tenured, newer-serving-Asian-
   market-clients).
4. **Create distinguishing visual device (keyline square)** — a keyline-square treatment
   over persona photography, applied exclusively to Constellation materials.
5. **Localize personas and materials for Mandarin/Cantonese markets** — personas
   themselves adapted (not merely translated) for Chinese-market segments.
6. **Produce multi-format applications** — advisor brochure, client kits, die-cut
   brochures, large-format prints, posters/one-pagers.
7. **Coordinate digital tool updates with external vendor** — the digital Constellation
   tool is vendor-maintained; rebrand rollout required explicit coordination outside the
   design team's direct control (an MVP approach was used for initial launch materials).
8. **Launch campaign** — launched January 6, 2020, in English/French plus partial
   Mandarin/Cantonese materials.

See `workflow_extraction.json` for full structured detail and `evidence_manifest.csv`
for quote-level evidence backing each step.

## Professional knowledge found

- A sub-brand operating inside a larger corporate identity needs its own consistent,
  minimal distinguishing device (the keyline square) rather than a full separate
  identity, to stay recognizable while remaining inside the parent brand system.
- Localization for a target-language market segment requires adapting persona
  representation itself, not merely translating base-language copy, when the underlying
  audience's demographic makeup differs from the base market.
- When a deliverable surface (here, a digital tool) is built/maintained by a third-party
  vendor outside the design team's direct control, rollout requires an explicit
  coordination step, distinct from owned print/collateral production; an MVP approach
  can be used to manage this constraint for initial launch materials.

## Important production/industry constraints

- Sub-brand must remain visually distinguishable from parent-brand collateral while
  staying within the parent identity system (named explicitly as one of three project
  "Challenges").
- Multilingual/multi-market localization requirement across 4 languages, with only some
  deliverables extended to the two additional languages (Mandarin/Cantonese).
- Dependency on an externally-maintained digital tool vendor requires an explicit
  design/content coordination step (also named as a core project "Challenge").
- A fixed hard launch date (January 6, 2020), under regulatory/merger-driven deadline
  pressure requiring "significant overtime hours" — confirmed as a named third challenge
  on re-verification; treated as PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED context (not a confirmed formal
  step) since it was not equally explicit across every original fetch pass.
